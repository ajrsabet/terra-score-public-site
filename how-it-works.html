How it works

Shared Footprint connects information about a product’s lifecycle to the evidence used to understand its impacts. Contributors can map the processes involved, link sources and measurements, and review the information used in an assessment.

The goal is not just to produce a score, but to show how a result was reached, what it covers, and where information is missing.

Map the lifecycle

A product’s footprint extends beyond the finished item. It includes the materials and processes involved in making it, transporting it, using it, and managing it at the end of its life. Shared Footprint will connect these stages so contributors can explore how they relate. A reusable bottle, for example, will be linked to the production of its materials, manufacturing, distribution, washing during use, and possible end-of-life paths.

Life cycle assessment (LCA) has evolved from its origins in energy analysis in the 1960s and 1970s into an approach widely used in research, industry, and policy to examine potential impacts across a product’s life. Shared Footprint builds on this established practice by linking lifecycle stages to the studies and evidence behind them, making it easier to trace what an assessment covers and where information is missing. See McManus and Taylor (2015).

LCA software is used in academic research as well as commercial practice. Examples include the open-source modeling tools openLCA and Brightway, and commercial products such as SimaPro, Sphera LCA for Experts, One Click LCA, and Umberto. They range from flexible modeling environments to more guided, sector-specific workflows, such as building assessments and environmental product declarations (EPDs). Software and data are separate: open-source tools can use licensed datasets, while commercial products may use proprietary, open, or user-provided data. That landscape makes room for an independent LCA platform. Shared Footprint has a foundationally different approach: it is a public decision making platform, open to all people. is being built to bring evidence from multiple sources into its own product-level assessments, apply a transparent impact framework across the lifecycle, and show how findings at each stage shape the result – rather than simply displaying or extending assessments produced elsewhere.

Connect the evidence

Each part of a lifecycle can be linked to information such as measurements, research, company data, and government records. Sources can describe a specific product or process, or provide broader data when more specific information is not available.

The platform will retain the context needed to interpret that information, including its source, scope, methods, and assumptions.

Review and assess

Contributions can include evidence, measurements, and proposals to change assessment categories or methods. Reviews consider the source, method, relevance, and applicability of the information. In the MVP, moderators make final decisions and record their rationale.

Over time, reviewer credibility will reflect contribution and review history. This score is intended to help identify and deter deliberate manipulation, while evidence and moderator review remain central to decisions.

Proposed changes to assessment rules and scoring weights are discussed by the community before a moderator records a decision. Accepted changes are versioned so assessments can be interpreted against the rules that produced them. A combined score is used only when an applicable model defines and approves its rules; otherwise, the assessment shows supported results and coverage by category.

Five impact areas and a governance dimension

The assessment framework covers five areas of impact, plus a separate dimension for organizational governance.

Climate

Climate effects associated with greenhouse gas emissions across a product’s lifecycle.

Water, Land, and Nature

Water impacts, land and soil, biodiversity, and ecosystem condition.

Equity

Social and economic impacts on people, including worker pay, rights, and livelihoods.

Resources and Circularity

Material and resource use, resource scarcity, waste, and circularity.

Health and Safety

Effects of products and processes on human health and safety.

Governance

How organizations make decisions, engage stakeholders, and demonstrate accountability. Assessed separately from physical impacts.

Show what's known and unknown

An assessment is only as useful as the information behind it. Shared Footprint will show where evidence is available, where estimates or assumptions are used, and where important gaps remain.

This helps readers distinguish a well-supported finding from an incomplete picture.

Follow an example

Consider a reusable water bottle. Its lifecycle might include producing its materials, manufacturing the bottle, transporting it, washing it between uses, and eventually recycling or disposing of it.

An assessment will connect evidence to each of those stages. If washing data were missing, for example, the platform will show that gap rather than presenting the result as a complete footprint.
