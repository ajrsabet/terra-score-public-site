What is Shared Footprint?

Shared Footprint is an environmental impact assessment tool to identify the environmental and social consequences of the products we make, use, and depend on. This platform provides a framework where the footprint of products and companies are scored.

Why do we need Shared Footprint?

Information about our impact is spread across scientific studies, government databases, company records, and organizations around the world.

  • Research examines materials, processes, and impacts – often in separate studies across different disciplines.
  • Government agencies collect data at national, regional, and local levels, across agencies and departments that may use different systems and standards.
  • Companies hold internal data about their materials, operations, and resource use – and, where available, their suppliers.

Each source offers a piece of the picture, but differences in scope, assumptions, and data quality make the pieces difficult to bring together.

Connecting them could help reveal where impacts occur across a product’s lifecycle, what drives them, and where better decisions could make a difference – while showing what is known and what remains uncertain.

How will Shared Footprint work?

Shared Footprint will map the lifecycle of products and companies, connecting information about materials, production, transportation, use, and end of life.

Researchers, companies, and individuals will be able to contribute information and sources. The platform will provide a way to review that evidence and assess its quality, so impact assessments can show what they’re based on and where gaps remain.

Why I'm doing this

I've spent much of my career working with complicated systems—designing buildings, managing construction projects, solving problems, and figuring out how different pieces fit together.

As I've studied environmental science and software development, I've become increasingly interested in a different kind of problem: how do we make sense of everything we already know about our impact on the planet — and actually use it to do better?
